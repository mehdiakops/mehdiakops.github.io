Expertise

Four areas that overlap more than most organisations expect. A findability problem is rarely solved by better writing, and a governance problem never is.

01

Help Center strategy

Owning the self-service surface end to end: what exists, how it is structured, how it is found, and how its performance is reported to leadership in business terms rather than publishing counts.

02

Information architecture

Most findability problems are structural, not editorial. I audit, restructure and govern content so that search, navigation and in-product links lead to the same right answer.

03

Content governance

Standards, templates, review cadences and the coaching that makes them stick. Writers ship faster when quality is defined rather than negotiated article by article.

04

Knowledge analytics

I instrument what I own, through Pendo, Google Tag Manager and platform APIs, then use search behaviour, exits, CTR and feedback signals to decide what gets fixed next.

How I work

Audit→ Prioritise→ Restructure→ Instrument→ Govern→ Measure

The order matters. Teams usually start at restructure, because rewriting feels like progress, and skip instrumentation entirely, which means nobody can prove the rewrite worked. I start with an audit so the work is aimed at what is actually broken, and I put measurement in before the changes rather than after, so the before-and-after is real rather than remembered.

About

I started in SaaS content seven years ago and moved steadily upstream. Writing taught me that most of the problems I was being asked to fix with better prose were not writing problems at all. An article nobody can find is an architecture problem. An article everyone finds and nobody finishes is a scoping problem. A team that rewrites the same page every quarter has a governance problem.

So I spend most of my time before the draft, and a fair amount after it, on the measurement that tells us whether any of it worked. At Motive I own Help Center strategy across a global customer base, report performance to leadership, and coach the writing team on standards that hold.

I also stay close to the technical layer. I instrument my own analytics, work directly with engineering on migrations and integrations, and build lightweight internal tools rather than wait a quarter for roadmap space. That combination, editorial judgement plus the ability to ship something, is the part of my work I find most useful and the part I am still expanding.
